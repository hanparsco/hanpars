Cooperation & Competition: China's API Industry on the World Stage

Published 24 June 2025 in Market Analysis.

Back to the archive
This article is kept with its original date and commentary. Market figures, forecasts and regulatory references reflect the time of writing and were not re-verified.

The global Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API) landscape is characterized by a complex interplay of cooperation and competition, with China occupying a central but evolving position. As geopolitical considerations increasingly influence pharmaceutical supply chains, China's API industry is navigating a changing international environment that presents both challenges and opportunities.

China-India API Dynamics

The relationship between the world's two largest API producers is marked by both competition and complementarity:

Comparative Advantages: China has maintained dominance in chemical synthesis and raw material production, controlling approximately 80% of global generic API supply by volume. India, meanwhile, has leveraged its FDA-approved facilities (the highest number outside the U.S.) and strong formulation capabilities to become the "pharmacy of the world" for finished dosage forms.

Strategic Shifts: India's Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme aims to boost domestic API production, targeting self-reliance by 2025 and reducing imports from China by 35%. This initiative represents one of the most significant attempts to reshape API supply chains globally.

Western Reshoring Initiatives

Developed economies are increasingly pursuing pharmaceutical supply chain diversification:

Policy Measures: Both the United States and European Union have implemented initiatives to encourage domestic API production, including the Inflation Reduction Act in the U.S. and the EU Pharmaceutical Strategy. These measures combine financial incentives, regulatory preferences, and strategic investments to reduce dependency on Chinese APIs.

The global API landscape is entering a period of significant recalibration, with China's dominant position being challenged by both competitive pressures and strategic diversification efforts. However, the fundamental advantages that enabled China's rise in API manufacturing—cost efficiency, chemical expertise, and manufacturing scale—remain largely intact.
